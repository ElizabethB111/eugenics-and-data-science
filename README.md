# Essay Summary: Ethics in Data Science and the Legacy of Eugenics

My essay examines the ethical responsibilities of data science by tracing connections between historical eugenics and modern algorithmic practices. Here's a summary of its main arguments:

## Core Thesis

The essay makes four central claims about data and ethics:

1. Data is meaningless without interpretation—it requires a narrative to have significance.
2. Every narrative reflects a particular viewpoint; true neutrality is impossible.
3. Data-driven narratives amplify both benefits and harms at a population level.
4. These realities demand daily ethical vigilance from data scientists.

## Historical Foundation

I explore how early statisticians like Francis Galton, Karl Pearson, and Ronald Fisher developed fundamental statistical tools (correlation, ANOVA) while simultaneously promoting eugenics. Galton's work exemplifies how data doesn't "speak for itself"—he interpreted correlations between prestigious relatives as proof of genetic superiority, ignoring environmental factors like wealth and education access.

## Counter-Narratives

I contrast these figures with Horace Mann Bond, who used similar data to argue that racial disparities in achievement reflected unequal access to resources rather than inherent inferiority, demonstrating how the same data can support vastly different narratives.

## Modern Parallels

I connect historical eugenics to contemporary algorithmic bias, particularly Joy Buolamwini's research showing facial recognition technology misidentifies dark-skinned females at higher rates—illustrating how unchecked technology can magnify harm across populations.

## Conclusion

I end with a call for diverse perspectives in data science, intellectual humility to admit mistakes, and careful progress that balances innovation with ethical responsibility.
